## What limits D and E

**Trigger reach matters most clearly for original E.** It fired on **33/180** official runs, and **48/180** E runs truncated without a program. Repair capacity also matters under the frozen harness. On selected development failures, property and oracle gates each detected **10/10**, yet all three D2 modes shared just **one distinct rescue**, and **11/18** distinct delivered trajectories looped. E2-review rescued **2/9** delivered cases. The same formula succeeded at one seed and failed at another. Clipped inputs, missing runtime diagnostics, and full-file rewrites make this evidence about the agent and harness together. [Official benchmark](<results/final-main/README.md:64>) · [Trajectory analysis](<enhanced_de/docs/agent_reports/2026-11-26_claude_trajectory_analysis.md:11>)

**The strongest case against that explanation is the held-out detection failure.** D2 and E2-review reached all **three** public-pass/hidden-fail candidates and delivered **zero** useful findings. Two wrong-answer defects escaped the checks, one scale defect exceeded the stress-input cap, and D2’s sole delivery flagged a judge-passing program at its stricter timeout. At least one E trigger was eligible on **8/9** A failures, so broad trigger eligibility alone bought little. A sound counterexample establishes a defect. It says little about coverage, localization, or whether the proposed repair is complete. On `abc387_c@2108`, adding localization and a repair to the same failing input coincided with a rescue. That makes artifact actionability a plausible causal factor, although one comparison cannot isolate it. Paid-provider agreement on **338 jointly answered inputs out of 360** across four banks establishes limited small-input agreement. Live API transfer remains unverified. ## Next benchmark and decisive experiments

**D-v2:** Carry one exploratory arm, **D2-property at the first public pass**, with postconditions, metamorphic checks, judge-calibrated stress tests, and one reproducible failure report. Keep stress-only and a sham gate with equal repair opportunity as controls. Drop the runtime oracle arm from the main comparison. Its offline **40/46 versus 38/46** mutant detections yielded no additional live rescue. **E-v2:** Drop strict and stall diagnosis as standalone contenders. Retain E-v1 as the historical control and test one plan-aware E policy with C. Use fresh tasks, multiple local seeds and independent frontier draws, a matched provider and budgets, and live policies. Report paired task-level pass@1, rescues and regressions, trigger and delivery rates, and cost. [D2 ablations](<enhanced_de/docs/02_PHASE0_AND_PHASE1_D2_FINDINGS.md:24>)

- **C+D:** Fork the same C trajectory at its first public pass into sham, property-gate feedback, and identical feedback with independently verified fault localization. Include silent gates in the overall comparison. This separates residual defect detection from the ability to repair after a useful finding. Official C still had **19/180** public-pass/hidden-fail attempts. [Official run data](<results/final-main/report/runs.csv>)
- **C+E:** Fork at **C’s own first public failure**. Compare a diagnosis that sees C’s plan, candidate and failure evidence with an equally long, identically formatted plan refresh that sees the statement and prior plan, plus a sham with the same remaining repair budget. Official C encountered public failure in **39/180** attempts. This tests whether access to failure evidence adds value beyond another dose of planning at the same point.
